**IsDB Geoinformatics Data Platform (GEIDA) · Operations Complex**

## Points covered

### GEIDA at its current stage

GEIDA has moved from concept to implementation and is being applied on an initial set of IsDB projects, with anchor projects in Azerbaijan, Mali and Uganda. The approach is to establish a reliable geolocation for a project — a boundary, a corridor, a command area, a catchment or a service area — and connect it to geospatial evidence that can then be used systematically across all phases of the project cycle.

### Where geospatial evidence enters the project cycle

| Stage | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Country strategy | Spatial diagnostics and baselines on climate and land use |
| Identification | Site screening, hazard exposure and accessibility checks |
| Appraisal & design | Standardised project maps and a documented baseline |
| Implementation | Remote supervision of physical progress between missions |
| Completion | Comparable before-and-after evidence over the same boundary |
| Evaluation | Independent verification of spatial change |

Each project phase carries its own reporting requirements — PCN, PPRR, PAD and RRM during approval and financing, then PASR and the Project Completion Report, alongside monitoring, evaluation and impact assessment. Geospatial evidence is prepared once against a standardised boundary and reused across these documents.

### Worked examples presented

- A school and cyclone-shelter programme, showing how mapped locations support verification and reporting against the intended results
- Irrigation performance before and after investment — crop area, production, crop water use and water productivity measured over time
- Land and water management contrasts visible across a shared border, illustrating what layered analysis reveals about a landscape
- Portfolio-level mapping, showing how projects and their boundaries can be viewed together across a programme

### From the eToolkit to the GEIDA platform

The eToolkit was developed with IsDB colleagues to bring Earth Observation data directly into project workflows, and was the platform used by Batch 1 participants to generate baseline reports for their own project sites. Work is underway on the GEIDA platform, which will integrate these capabilities with automated workflows.

### Capacity development and institutionalisation

The certification programme runs in cohorts: Batch 1 in July 2026, Batch 2 in September 2026 followed by three online sessions, and Batch 3 during the GPP Knowledge Week. The knowledge hub — this site — keeps the training material available for staff joining later or revisiting a topic.

Alongside the training, the briefing addressed the human-resource dimension: sustaining the capability inside the Bank requires internal skills and internal resources rather than continuing reliance on external providers.

### Discussion

Questions from participants focused on the boundary between what satellite data can evidence and what it cannot. Points raised included the need to verify the accuracy of satellite-derived information against field knowledge; that conditions such as maintenance quality and beneficiary experience require engagement with ministries and communities; and that infrastructure indicators are more challenging to capture remotely than land and water indicators. A participant case study on a transport project, prepared during Batch 1, was presented as an illustration of using EO-derived change as one supporting line of evidence among others.
